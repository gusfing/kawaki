Website Launch QA Checklist: What to Test Before Going Live (50-Point Engineering Audit)

KS
Kunal Sharma Founder & Principal Engineer, Kawaki Studios

Executive Summary / Key Takeaways

The definitive 50-point website launch QA checklist: Verify technical SEO, Core Web Vitals, form webhooks, SSL certificates, and zero-downtime DNS cutover.

  • Comprehensive technical architecture analysis engineered specifically for growing organizations.
  • Clear decision frameworks, trade-off matrices, and benchmark criteria for executive evaluation.
  • Production-grade implementation guidance focused on long-term scalability and security.

Flipping the switch on a newly redesigned website or digital flagship is one of the most high-stakes moments in digital business. Months of creative design, architecture, and content production culminate in a single deployment event.

Yet, without a rigorous, multi-disciplinary pre-launch quality assurance (QA) protocol, even the most visually stunning websites stumble out of the gate:

  • Broken inquiry forms that fail to notify sales teams.
  • Missing 301 redirect trees that destroy hard-won organic search rankings overnight.
  • Sluggish mobile viewports that trigger high bounce rates.
  • Misconfigured DNS records that take email servers offline during cutover.

The Engineering Golden Rule

Launching a website is an orchestrated systems cutover, not a simple button click. Top-tier engineering studios do not rely on subjective visual inspections. They execute a deterministic, item-by-item technical audit across performance, SEO, security, cross-device responsiveness, and analytics before production traffic touches the server.

At Kawaki Studios, we engineer and launch mission-critical digital flagships and custom web platforms from our studio in New Delhi. In this comprehensive guide, we publish our field-tested 50-Point Website Launch QA Checklist to ensure your next release is smooth, secure, and commercially successful.

1. Category I: Technical SEO & Crawlability (Points 1–10)

Search engine optimization must be verified before launch, not diagnosed after traffic drops:

  1. Self-Referencing Canonical Tags: Verify every single indexable page carries a fully qualified, absolute canonical URL matching your primary domain (https://kawaki.co.in/page).
  2. Robots.txt Configuration: Confirm that the production robots.txt does not accidentally carry Disallow: / left over from staging environments.
  3. XML Sitemap Accuracy: Ensure sitemap.xml includes all canonical, indexable URLs and excludes 301 redirects, 404 pages, staging URLs, or password-protected client portals.
  4. Comprehensive 301 Redirect Tree: Run automated crawl comparisons between your legacy website and new URLs. Confirm every legacy URL maps directly to its corresponding replacement with a 301 permanent redirect.
  5. Single H1 per Page: Confirm every page possesses exactly one semantic <h1> tag matching user search intent, supported by logical <h2> and <h3> heading hierarchies.
  6. Title Tag & Meta Description Uniqueness: Ensure zero duplicate title tags or generic placeholder descriptions ("Home", "About Us") exist across the site.
  7. Semantic Schema.org JSON-LD Structured Data: Validate Organization, WebSite, BreadcrumbList, and FAQPage schemas using Google Rich Results validator.
  8. Noindex Directives on Internal Search & Admin: Confirm that internal administrative areas, staging parameters, and dynamic search filter query strings carry noindex, follow.
  9. Image Alt Text Completeness: Ensure all informative images carry descriptive, human-readable alt attributes, while purely decorative backgrounds use empty alt attributes (alt="").
  10. Favicon and Web App Manifest: Verify complete favicon coverage (favicon.ico, favicon.svg, apple-touch-icon.png, site.webmanifest).

2. Category II: Performance & Core Web Vitals (Points 11–20)

Google directly benchmarks mobile user experience and Core Web Vitals when determining search rankings:

  1. Largest Contentful Paint (LCP) Under 2.0s: Ensure the main hero headline or visual asset renders in under 2.0 seconds on throttled 4G mobile connections.
  2. Interaction to Next Paint (INP) Under 150ms: Test interactive elements (accordions, mobile navigation menus, filter tabs) to guarantee instantaneous UI response.
  3. Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS) Under 0.05: Confirm explicit width and height dimensions on all images, video wrappers, and custom embeds to prevent jarring content jumps during asset load.
  4. Critical Font Subsetting & Display Swap: Verify font files are stripped of unused glyphs, preloaded in the document head, and configured with font-display: swap to prevent Flash of Invisible Text (FOIT).
  5. Modern Image Formatting (WebP / AVIF): Confirm all raster photography is compressed and served in modern formats, eliminating heavy legacy PNG/JPEG payloads.
  6. Responsive Image Sizing: Verify srcset and sizes attributes serve scaled images matched to mobile viewports rather than full-resolution desktop assets.
  7. JavaScript Bundle Splitting: Verify client-side code is divided into modular chunks so users only download code required for the current viewport.
  8. CSS Tree-Shaking: Ensure dead CSS from unused component templates is purged before production asset bundling.
  9. Edge CDN Caching Policies: Verify static assets serve with aggressive Cache-Control: public, max-age=31536000, immutable headers.
  10. Zero Render-Blocking Third-Party Scripts: Ensure analytics, live chat widgets, and marketing tags load asynchronously (async or defer) without stalling the primary DOM parser.

3. Category III: Forms, APIs, and Workflow Automations (Points 21–30)

Forms and interactive funnels represent the commercial heartbeat of your platform:

  1. End-to-End Form Submissions: Submit live test entries through every form on the site (contact forms, newsletter signups, quotation calculators, consultation schedulers).
  2. Client-Side Input Validation: Test edge cases (invalid email syntax, missing phone digits, blank required fields) to ensure clear, accessible error messages render inline.
  3. Server-Side Sanitization: Verify inputs are scrubbed and sanitized against SQL injection, cross-site scripting (XSS), and malicious payload attacks.
  4. CRM & Webhook Ingestion: Confirm lead submissions successfully transmit to your customer relationship management (CRM) software or notification endpoints.
  5. Admin Notification Emails: Verify internal team notification alerts arrive instantly with complete lead details and accurate submission timestamps.
  6. User Confirmation Emails: Check transactional auto-responder emails to ensure branding, links, and tone of voice reflect your studio standards.
  7. Spam Defense (reCAPTCHA / Turnstile / Honeypot): Verify automated spam bots are blocked without frustrating legitimate users with difficult visual captchas.
  8. Form Loading & Success States: Ensure submit buttons display a clear loading spinner during network transit and transition to an unambiguous success confirmation message.
  9. Error Boundary Handling: Simulate a network drop or server failure during form submission to confirm the interface displays a graceful fallback without crashing.
  10. Webhook Idempotency: Confirm that double-clicking a submit button or rapid repeated submissions do not produce duplicate CRM leads or double charges.

4. Category IV: Cross-Browser & Device Responsiveness (Points 31–40)

A website must provide a flawless user experience across all screen sizes and operating systems:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ CROSS-DEVICE VERIFICATION MATRIX │
├──────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. Mobile iOS Safari │ Notch adaptation, bottom address bar bounce │
│ 2. Mobile Android Chrome │ Dynamic viewport height (100dvh), touch hits│
│ 3. Tablet Portrait/Land │ Navigation collapse, split column balance │
│ 4. Desktop Windows/Mac │ Font smoothing, scrollbar gutter stability │
│ 5. Ultra-Wide Displays │ Container max-width constraints, margins │
└──────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

  1. Physical Mobile Device Testing: Test on real iOS (Safari) and Android (Chrome) devices rather than relying solely on desktop browser developer emulators.
  2. Dynamic Viewport Height (100dvh): Verify full-height hero sections adapt cleanly when mobile browser address bars expand and collapse during scroll.
  3. Touch Target Sizing: Ensure all buttons, links, and navigation items maintain minimum touch targets of at least 44 × 44 pixels with adequate hit spacing.
  4. Mobile Navigation Drawer Mechanics: Test mobile menu opening, closing, scroll-locking, and escape key / tap-outside dismissal.
  5. Horizontal Scroll Overflow Prevention: Verify that zero unintended horizontal scrolling (overflow-x) occurs at standard mobile widths (320px, 375px, 414px).
  6. Cross-Browser Parity: Inspect layout rendering across Apple Safari, Google Chrome, Mozilla Firefox, and Microsoft Edge.
  7. High-DPI / Retina Asset Clarity: Confirm vector logos (SVG) and brand imagery render crisply on high-pixel-density displays without pixelation.
  8. Table and Data Grid Responsiveness: Ensure wide data tables collapse into swipeable cards or horizontal scrolling containers on narrow mobile viewports.
  9. Accessibility & Keyboard Navigation: Verify logical Tab focus order, visible focus rings, and screen-reader accessibility across interactive components.
  10. Color Contrast Compliance: Verify body copy and button labels pass contrast ratios against background colors to ensure readable text for all visitors.

5. Category V: Security, Analytics, and DNS Cutover (Points 41–50)

The final ten checkpoints govern infrastructure safety and launch-day cutover protocols:

  1. SSL / TLS Certificate Provisioning: Verify a valid, auto-renewing SSL certificate is active on the root domain and all subdomains (https://kawaki.co.in/).
  2. HTTP to HTTPS Force Redirect: Confirm all insecure http:// traffic automatically forces a 301 permanent redirect to https://.
  3. Non-WWW vs WWW Domain Normalization: Ensure your secondary domain format automatically 301-redirects to your primary canonical domain to prevent duplicate content reporting.
  4. Security Response Headers: Verify modern security headers are configured (Strict-Transport-Security, X-Content-Type-Options, X-Frame-Options, Content-Security-Policy).
  5. Custom 404 Error Page: Verify a branded, helpful 404 page exists with clear links back to your homepage, services, and search functionality.
  6. Analytics & Tag Governance: Confirm tracking scripts record real-time pageviews and conversion events correctly in staging before flipping production DNS.
  7. DNS TTL Lowering Pre-Launch: Lower your DNS Time-to-Live (TTL) to 300 seconds (5 minutes) at least 48 hours prior to launch to enable rapid rollback if unexpected issues emerge.
  8. MX & Email Record Preservation: When modifying root DNS A or CNAME records, verify your Google Workspace or Microsoft 365 MX records remain untouched so team email never drops.
  9. Post-Launch Rapid Indexing Ping: Submit new canonical URLs to Google Indexing API and IndexNow immediately after cutover to accelerate bot discovery.
  10. Google Search Console Ownership: Confirm your GSC domain property is verified, submit your new sitemap.xml, and monitor indexing coverage logs daily for the first two weeks.

6. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

When should a website launch QA audit begin?

QA is not an afterthought squeezed into the final hour before launch. Pre-launch checks should begin at least 7 to 10 days before the scheduled cutover date, allowing adequate runway for developers to resolve cross-device bugs, configure 301 redirect trees, and verify third-party webhook integrations.

What is the best day of the week to launch a website?

The best days to launch a business website are Tuesday or Wednesday morning. Avoid Friday afternoon or weekend launches; if an unexpected DNS propagation delay or checkout webhook error occurs, senior engineering and support teams should be fully available during regular business hours to diagnose and resolve it immediately.

What happens if we discover a critical bug after going live?

Having lowered your DNS TTL prior to launch, you can execute a rapid zero-downtime rollback to your legacy platform within minutes if an unresolvable checkout or data corruption issue emerges, allowing developers to isolate and fix the bug on staging.

Does launching a redesign temporarily drop Google rankings?

A brief organic fluctuation of 5% to 10% can occur over the first 7 to 14 days as search engine crawlers re-index updated page structures. However, if your 301 redirect tree is complete, canonical tags are accurate, and Core Web Vitals improve, organic search visibility will stabilize and typically outpace pre-launch baselines.

Conclusion: Launch with Uncompromising Confidence

A website launch should be an occasion for celebration, not panic. By enforcing a deterministic, 50-point engineering checklist, you protect your hard-won search rankings, safeguard conversion funnels, and present an investable, high-performance digital presence to the world.
